Why chunk bursts spike ping on TCP

Standard Minecraft servers push chunks, light updates, and KeepAlive pings into one FIFO queue and an uncapped OS send buffer. Small packets wait behind queued chunk bytes.

Vanilla Netty FIFO Head-of-line blocked
Chunk 0..15 (512 KB) LightUpdate KeepAlive StateSync

KeepAlive and state-sync packets wait behind hundreds of kilobytes of queued chunk data in user space and the kernel send queue.

Celeris Express + Kernel Clamp Bundle-safe preemption
KeepAlive StateSync ChunkBatchStart Chunk 0..N (32 KB slices)

Express traffic cuts ahead between 32 KB bulk slices while keeping BundleDelimiter trains intact. Linux TCP_NOTSENT_LOWAT=16KB caps kernel buffer bloat.

Where Vanilla TCP Matches Celeris

Packet scheduling cannot change physics or fix a slow main thread. These workloads stay unchanged.

Idle Connections

When no chunk burst sits in the queue, loopback KeepAlive P50 is 0.59 ms on Vanilla FIFO and 0.72 ms on Celeris Rust.

Geographic Fiber Distance

From Morocco to an EU host (roxaura.mcsh.gg), base mcPing stays 136 ms. Celeris prevents chunk floods from adding spikes on top of that base RTT.

Main-Thread TPS Lag

When entity AI or worldgen pulls TPS below 20, Netty transport changes do not speed up tick computation.
